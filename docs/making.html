Building the CARS Catalog: From Absolute to High-Precision Relative Locations

The Catalog of Relative Seismic locations (CARS) provides an enhanced, high-resolution imaging of Italian seismicity spanning from 1981 to 2018. The following steps outline the methodological framework used to construct the catalog, evolving from the absolute CLASS baseline to sub-kilometric precision.

1. The Absolute Baseline (CLASS)

The foundation of CARS is the 3D absolute CLASS catalog, which established a robust baseline of approximately 420,000 earthquakes across the Italian peninsula. This initial dataset integrated historical readings and modern, centralized bulletins, relying on a comprehensive 3D velocity model to bridge regional and local monitoring.

2. Compiling Differential Travel Times

To feed the double-difference (DD) inversion pipeline, a massive dataset was derived from the absolute baseline. We compiled approximately 34 million P-wave and 24 million S-wave catalog differential travel times, carefully managing network evolution and applying rigorous station aliasing procedures to ensure consistency.

3. Integration of Waveform Cross-Correlations

For the modern instrumental era (post-2009), we significantly improved timing resolution by computing over two billion time-domain waveform cross-correlations. This yielded roughly 40 million high-precision differential delay measurements (CC ≥ 0.7) for event pairs within a 10 km distance, achieving sub-millisecond precision and dramatically minimizing manual picking scatter.

4. Velocity Structure Segmentation

To accurately account for Italy's complex regional geology during the relative location process, the territory was segmented into 17 distinct seismotectonic macro-areas. Each region was assigned a unique, localized 1D mean velocity profile extracted from a national-scale 3D tomographic model, ensuring that the inversion remained tightly constrained by the local structure.

5. Strategic Initial Location Blending

We optimized the starting coordinates using a dual-integration framework. For high-density, critical areas like the 2009 L'Aquila or 2016 Amatrice sequences, we utilized highly refined 1D absolute locations. Conversely, for scattered background seismicity, the original 3D CLASS coordinates were retained, maximizing the resolution of the inputs prior to DD relocation.

6. Spatial Gridding Strategy

The vast dataset posed severe computational challenges, particularly memory exhaustion during inversion. We circumvented this by partitioning the entire study area into uniform 100 km x 100 km computational grid cells. To preserve structural continuity across boundaries, a robust 80% spatial overlap in both longitude and latitude was enforced.

7. High-Density Sub-sampling Workflow

In highly clustered regions like the Central Apennines, where event-pair links grow quadratically, standard gridding was insufficient. Here, we deployed a random sub-sampling scheme within the overlapping cells, creating 20 numerically stable batches of 10,000 events each. This ensured consistent spatial connectivity and computational feasibility.

8. Parallel Double-Difference Relocation

With the data structurally partitioned, each overlapping grid cell and sub-sampled batch was independently relocated using the hypoDD algorithm. This parallelized approach systematically minimized path-dependent velocity variations and effectively reduced the residuals across millions of equations.

9. Catalog Synthesis and Merging

Following the independent relocations, the comprehensive CARS catalog was synthesized. Hypocenters from overlapping boundaries and multiple sub-sampled batches were merged by calculating a linear-weighted mean. This weighting was based on the normalized distances of each solution to its cluster centroid, guaranteeing a continuous, high-resolution final dataset.

10. Parametric Bootstrap Error Estimation

To rigorously quantify uncertainties, we applied a space-partitioned parametric bootstrap scheme. By extracting residual standard deviations for each cell, we generated 100 randomized datasets per cell. Relocating these produced approximately 673 million repeated locations, allowing us to define robust horizontal and vertical errors based on the final standard deviations.

11. The Final CARS Dataset

The resulting CARS catalog contains ~320,000 highly resolved hypocenters. The relative relocation successfully canceled common-path errors, notably correcting a systematic 2.5 km over-deepening artifact present in the absolute baseline for shallow crustal events. CARS now provides unprecedented, sharp 3D imaging of Italy's active fault architectures.
